🌊 slow tide

Slow, quiet instrumental songs for settling a baby to sleep. Each one is written to rules drawn from published research, and each song's page shows which studies it relies on. They may help your baby settle; nothing here is a treatment.

Before you press play
  • Put the speaker at least 2 metres from the crib. Never in it or on it.
  • Keep the volume low: at the baby's ear it should be quieter than a calm conversation (under about 50 dB; a phone sound-meter app will tell you). Set it once, with the loudest song.
  • Use the sleep timer instead of playing all night. It fades out over a minute.

Why: consumer sleep machines have been measured above hazardous levels at crib distance (Hugh et al., Pediatrics 2014); the AAP's ceiling for infant environments is 45 dB and its 2023 noise policy asks parents to use sleep sound at low volume and limited duration. Sources are in the research notes.

How the songs are made

An agent surveys the literature on infant sleep and infant-directed song, keeps the findings in RESEARCH.md, distils them into numbered rules in COMPOSITION_RULES.md, and writes each song in strudel against those rules. A checker script rejects a song that breaks a rule or cites a study that is not in the notes, and the lab measures the audio for clicks, clipping and level.

Songs play in your browser with strudel; nothing is uploaded.
